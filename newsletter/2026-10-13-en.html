The house that keeps getting cheaper

Edition 1 | Tuesday 13 October 2026

This week, from Tomás

Welcome to the first one. Every Tuesday we go through about 27,000 properties for sale and for rent in Paraguay and tell you what actually happened: which ones to look at, what things cost, where the new listings are showing up, and what a sensible offer looks like. No sales pitch. Nobody pays to be in here.

This week the big story is a house in San Vicente that has cut its price four times since August, and a sale on the same spot that nobody can explain. Also: the dollar quietly moved every guaraní price in the country, the barrios with the most new listings, and a flat you get to price yourself.

The house of the week

A four-bedroom house in San Vicente, Asunción

From Nidia, corredora. San Vicente, Asunción. Four bedrooms, three bathrooms, 200 m2 built, on a quiet street of low walls and old trees. Asking Gs 560 million, about USD 98,000.

Now the part that made me look twice. Listed at the end of August. Cut on 3 September. Cut again nine days later. Cut on 1 October. Cut once more on 9 October. The price is now 22% below where it started. I have sat across from that seller many times. Either he needs the money, or his broker got the first price wrong and is walking it down in public. Both are good news for a buyer.

What is it worth? Our estimate says about USD 130,000 for a house like this in this part of town, and the estimate is usually within about 20 to 30%. So the asking price sits well below what similar homes have sold for.

One catch, and it is a real one. A house on this exact spot was reported sold on 16 September for Gs 750 million. Same house, still listed after a sale fell through at the escribanía? The neighbour, dropped on the same map pin by a lazy broker? I have seen both. Ask that question before you ask about the roof.

What I would offer: somewhere between Gs 470 and 525 million, which is where past sales in Asunción landed against their asking prices. What USD 100,000 buys right now

From Teodoro, who keeps the numbers. In Central, USD 100,000 is the middle of the market for a house: half of all houses for sale there ask between about USD 70,000 and USD 167,000, and the typical one asks about USD 105,000. In Asunción the same money gets you a flat, where the typical asking price is about USD 112,000. A typical house in Asunción asks about USD 290,000.

Three more things worth knowing before you make an offer:

  • Four in ten sales reported by RE/MAX brokers over the last year closed at the full asking price. The rest closed about 7% under. If you want a discount, look for homes that have already cut or have sat for months.
  • Half the houses for sale have been listed for more than six months. A fresh listing is not where the bargaining happens.
  • House prices in Central looked 3% higher this week than last. They were not. Four in five sellers there price in guaraníes, and the guaraní gained 3% against the dollar. The sellers changed nothing. If you earn in dollars, every guaraní-priced home got dearer for you anyway.

Renting

From Maren, who rented here for three years before buying. My first lease was in guaraníes and my salary was in dollars. Every month the rent was a different number in my head, and my landlord, a lovely woman with a parrot, never changed a thing.

That happened to a lot of people last week. A typical flat in Asunción asks about USD 1,000 a month, half of them between USD 790 and USD 1,320, and most of those adverts are written in guaraníes. A Gs 5.7 million rent was about USD 970 a week ago. Now it is about USD 1,000. Same flat, same parrot. In Central a flat asks about USD 600, and a house in Asunción about USD 2,350.

What it means for you: before you sign, ask one sentence out loud: "Is the rent fixed in guaraníes or in dollars?" Then check it against the currency you get paid in. If the two do not match, your rent moves every week, whether anyone tells you or not.

One for the dinner table

From Teodoro. In October 2025, the latest month the state has published, 4,512 properties changed hands in Paraguay, 2% more than a year earlier, and 11% more in Asunción. The registry counts every sale and never tells you the price. We count the prices. Between us, you get almost a market.
