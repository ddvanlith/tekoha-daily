The flat that asks a third less

Edition 2 | Tuesday 20 October 2026

This week, from Tomás

The big story is a small flat: two bedrooms in San Vicente, asking about a third less than our estimate, with a neighbour about 300 metres away that sold for more in August. Nidia has the question to ask first.

Also inside: the deepest cuts we trust, the twelve brokers whose listings vanish and come back (promised last week), landlords cutting rents, and a house in Sajonia to guess. The barrios with the dollar taken out wait a week: one source went quiet, and we do not print a comparison with a gap in it.

The house of the week

A two-bedroom flat in San Vicente, Asunción

From Nidia, corredora. San Vicente, Asunción. Two bedrooms, two bathrooms, 81 m2 in all. Asking Gs 450 million, about USD 78,500.

It went up on the portal on 10 August. When we first saw it, it asked Gs 500 million. On 16 October the owner took 10% off. Ten weeks, one cut, no drama. That is the kind of seller I like: someone who listens.

What is it worth? Our estimate says about USD 121,000, and for flats in Asunción it is usually within about 20%. The asking price sits about a third below that. The neighbours side with the estimate: an 80 m2 flat 300 metres away was reported sold in August for Gs 465 million, and a 94 m2 flat about a kilometre away sold on 12 October for Gs 520 million.

The catch, because a price this kind always has one: the advert says 81 m2, but the same page also carries 67 m2. Ask which is the covered area you will live in, which floor it is on, and what the expensas are, the monthly building charge on top. If the answer is 81 m2 on a decent floor, do not haggle.

What I would offer: between Gs 432 and 450 million. Almost half the flats in Asunción that sold in the last year went at the full asking price, and the rest usually gave up about 7%. What USD 100,000 buys right now

From Teodoro, who keeps the numbers. In Central, USD 100,000 is the middle of the house market: the typical house asks about USD 105,000, and half ask between about USD 69,000 and USD 167,000. A flat in Central asks about USD 78,000. In Asunción the same money buys a flat, not a house: the typical flat asks about USD 112,000, and one in four asks under USD 80,000, like Nidia's. The typical house in Asunción asks about USD 290,000.

Three things to know before you make an offer:

  • Four in ten sales reported by RE/MAX brokers over the last year closed at the full asking price. The rest closed about 7% under. Houses in Asunción are where the haggling lives: the typical one closed about 6% under its last price.
  • Half the houses for sale in Paraguay have been listed for about seven months or more. Time favours the buyer, but only on the old listings.
  • Twelve brokers in our count habitually take a listing down and put it back up within two months. They hold about 300 listings, a little over one in a hundred. Their listings end in a sale or a price cut about one time in five; everyone else's, about one time in two. A home that keeps coming back is not about to move.

Renting

From Maren, who rented here for three years before buying. The flat across the hall from mine in Villa Morra sat empty a whole summer. In March the sign came down. The rent had come down first.

Landlords are doing that now. They cut asking rents 40 times last week, up from 26, usually by about 9%. In Central it was about one rental in forty in a single week, more than double a usual week there.

A typical flat in Asunción asks about USD 975 a month, half between USD 750 and USD 1,300, most of them priced in guaraníes. In Central a flat asks about USD 580. A house asks about USD 2,350 in Asunción, about USD 700 in Central. Of the rentals that close, three in four go at the asking rent; the rest settle about 8% lower.

What it means for you: before you sign, ask "How long has this flat been empty?" If the answer is in months, ask for the discount out loud. Almost one rental in four closes below the asking rent, and nobody offers it first.

One for the dinner table

From Teodoro. The national cadastre keeps its own price for every square metre in the country, the fiscal value. In Asunción this month, a typical listing asks about eight times that fiscal value: about five and a half times for a house, nine and a half for a flat, thirteen and a half for bare land. In Central, land asks about 35 times. Neither is what a home sells for. But the next time someone says the tax on their house is too high, you know how far behind the state is.
