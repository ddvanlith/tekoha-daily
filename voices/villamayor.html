PARA VOS

La rebaja es el dato chico. La espera es el dato grande.

Sobre 1,061 ventas con cierre reportado por la red RE/MAX, la mediana del descuento contra el último precio publicado es 3.0%. Un cuarto de las operaciones cerró al precio pedido o por encima.

Por qué importa: si estás por publicar, el precio que vas a defender se mueve poco. Lo que se mueve es la cantidad de días, y esa cantidad depende del tipo de inmueble.

En números (avisos activos de venta, días desde la primera publicación):

  • Terreno: mediana 134 días, percentil 75 en 411. n=5,838.
  • Dúplex: mediana 215 días, percentil 75 en 470. n=273.
  • Casa: mediana 77 días, percentil 75 en 214. n=4,479.
  • Departamento: mediana 77 días, percentil 75 en 215. n=2,897.

El dato raro: en los ocho cierres más recientes en la misma moneda que publicamos esta semana, los días publicados no explicaron el descuento. Un terreno en Cambyretá con 2,139 días cerró al precio pedido, Gs 260,000,000. Otro en Ciudad del Este con 1 día también cerró al pedido, Gs 600,000,000. El mayor descuento, 6.0%, fue un local en Lambaré con 516 días. n=8. Es una pregunta, no una conclusión.

Qué sigue: la mediana por barrio llega con la geocodificación. Hasta entonces, la comparación válida es contra tu tipo de inmueble a nivel país.

En resumen: antes de bajar el precio, fijate cuántos días llevás publicado contra la mediana de tu tipo. Si estás por debajo, el mercado todavía no te contestó.

THE UNDERWRITE

The negotiation in this market is not happening on price: half of the sales the broker panel reports close within 3.0% of the terminal asking price and a quarter close at or above it, so the cost of a position here is carry, measured in days, not discount.

  • Ask-to-close, sales: P50 -3.0%. n=1,061, broker-reported, RE/MAX panel, terminal ask, same-currency only.
  • Distribution: P25 -8.6% / P75 0.0%. Same set.

  • Staleness, active sale stock: terreno P50 134 days / P75 411. n=5,838, full-universe sources.
  • vs 1 year ago: not available. Series begins 2026-08.

By the numbers: a quarter of closes land at or above asking; a quarter land at -8.6% or worse. The 8.6-point interquartile range, not the median, is the underwriting input.

Between the lines: half of active terreno listings are 134 days old and the top quartile is past 411; duplex stock runs 215 and 470. A land position is underwritten on time to liquidity, not on negotiating room.

Reality check: today's data cannot tell you whether age predicts discount. In the eight most recent same-currency closes, a Cambyretá lot published 2,139 days closed at full ask and a Ciudad del Este lot published one day closed at full ask. n=8. Recorded as a question, not a finding.

What's next: zone-level staleness with barrio geocoding. Until it lands, the table is a country-level input.

LIVING HERE

Nobody telephones a tenant to say the asking rent downstairs has come down.

Five rent cuts is what the system caught in 26 hours.

For context: rental asking prices here do not reprice on a schedule. An owner holds the number until the unit has sat empty, then cuts once. Five owners cutting in one window is a cluster, not a trend line.

You do not have to be buying anything for this to reach your rent.

  • The closed median is a floor, not your barrio. 19 rentals closed in the past week at a median of $634 a month, per the broker panel, country-wide, every size.
  • Old listings answer the phone. Half of active departamentos (a departamento is a flat in a building; expensas, the monthly building charge, come on top of the rent) have been published 77 days, a quarter past 215.
  • The sale cuts are land. Six of seven on today's board are terrenos (a terreno is a bare plot, sold with nothing on it), including one in Areguá from about $17,500 to $15,000, asking.

What it means for you: at renewal, ask how long the vacant unit nearest yours has been listed before you discuss price. Days published is the leverage; the median is only the floor.

Watch for: the count tomorrow. Five in a day is a cluster; five a day for a week is a repricing.
